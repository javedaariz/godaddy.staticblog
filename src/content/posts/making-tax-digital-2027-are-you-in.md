## The three dates and limits

HMRC is bringing Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax in by income band. Which group you are in depends on the income shown on an earlier tax return, not on this year's earnings.

| Start date | Qualifying income limit | Which tax return is tested |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 April 2026 | More than £50,000 | 2024-25 |
| 6 April 2027 | More than £30,000 | 2025-26 |
| 6 April 2028 | More than £20,000 | 2026-27 |

## What counts as qualifying income

Qualifying income is your **gross** income from self-employment and property, added together. "Gross" means turnover before you take off expenses, so a freelancer with £32,000 of invoices and £9,000 of costs is still over £30,000. Salary, pensions, dividends and savings interest do not count towards the test.

## A quick way to test yourself

1. Open your 2025-26 Self Assessment return. You file it by 31 January 2027.
2. Add your self-employment turnover and your gross property income.
3. If the total is more than £30,000, you join MTD on 6 April 2027.
4. If it is between £20,000 and £30,000, plan for 6 April 2028.

HMRC may write to you if it thinks you are in scope. Do not wait for a letter, because the rules apply whether or not you receive one.

## What changes if you are in

- You keep digital records of income and expenses using compatible software from 6 April 2027.
- You send HMRC a short update every quarter instead of one annual summary. See the [MTD quarterly deadlines for 2027-28](/freelancer-finance/mtd-quarterly-deadlines-2027-28/).
- You still make a final declaration after the year ends.
- You do not pay tax quarterly. Payment dates stay as they are.

## What to do before April

- Work out which group you are in using the steps above.
- Choose software that works with MTD. HMRC publishes a software finder on GOV.UK.
- Start recording income and expenses digitally from the first day of the new tax year.

## Edge cases to check on GOV.UK

The exact wording of the limit, how joint income and partnerships are treated, and exemptions are set out in the official guidance. This article is a summary, so confirm anything unusual about your situation on GOV.UK or with an accountant.

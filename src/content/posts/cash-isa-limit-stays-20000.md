## What was planned, and what changed

The previous Chancellor announced at the November 2025 Budget that, from April 2027, savers under 65 could put only £12,000 a year into cash ISAs. The other £8,000 of the £20,000 overall ISA allowance would have been for investments.

At the 28 October 2026 Budget, Chancellor John Healey scrapped that. The limit stays at £20,000 for everyone, and the overall ISA allowance is unchanged at £20,000.

## What has not changed

- The overall ISA allowance is £20,000 a year, which you can split across cash and investment ISAs.
- Savers aged 65 and over were always going to keep the full cash allowance.

## What you do not need to do

Some articles written before the Budget urged savers to fill a cash ISA before April 2027. That advice no longer applies. There is no rule change forcing you to move cash into an ISA by a deadline.

## What the Budget did change for savers

Tax on savings interest outside ISAs is going up from 6 April 2027. That makes an ISA more valuable for people with larger cash balances. See [tax on savings interest from April 2027](/money/tax-on-savings-interest-april-2027/).

## New "starter" investment ISA

The Chancellor also announced plans, working with the sector, for a new starter ISA to make investing more accessible. At the time of writing the details have not been published, so we will update this page when they are.

## When the change becomes law

Deloitte's summary says the £20,000 limit will be retained through secondary legislation due to be laid before Parliament in November 2026. Check GOV.UK for confirmation before April.

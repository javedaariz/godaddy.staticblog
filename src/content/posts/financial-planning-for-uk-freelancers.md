## 1. Track income and expenses

Record every payment and business cost as it happens. Good records make tax easier, and from April 2027 many freelancers must keep them digitally. See [Making Tax Digital: are you in?](/freelancer-finance/making-tax-digital-2027-are-you-in/)

## 2. Separate personal and business money

A separate business account makes bookkeeping simpler and shows what the business really earns.

## 3. Set money aside for tax

As a sole trader you are responsible for your own tax. Move a share of each payment into a savings pot so the bill does not come as a surprise. Your own figure depends on your income and costs, so ask an accountant or use HMRC's guidance to work out a sensible amount.

## 4. Know your Self Assessment dates

You file a Self Assessment return and pay tax on set dates, including 31 January for the return and balancing payment. Some people also make payments on account. Check the dates for your situation on GOV.UK.

## 5. Build an emergency fund

A common target is three to six months of living costs in an easy-access account. Freelance income can drop between projects.

## 6. Budget on your lowest months

Plan your spending around a cautious income estimate rather than your best month.

## 7. Use invoicing tools and chase late payments

Invoicing software with automatic reminders reduces late payment.

## 8. Plan for sickness and time off

Self-employed people do not get employer sick pay. Think about savings, and insurance such as income protection, that would cover you if you could not work.

## 9. Save for retirement

Self-employed people do not get workplace pension contributions. Consider a personal pension or SIPP, and use your ISA allowance. See our [money and savings guides](/money/).

## 10. Review every quarter

Check income, expenses and goals each quarter. That habit also fits well with Making Tax Digital's [quarterly updates](/freelancer-finance/mtd-quarterly-deadlines-2027-28/).

These dates are for people who join MTD for Income Tax on **6 April 2027** because their 2025-26 qualifying income was more than £30,000. Not sure you are in? Start with [are you in Making Tax Digital?](/freelancer-finance/making-tax-digital-2027-are-you-in/)

## Your 2027-28 quarterly update dates

| Update | Period it covers (standard) | Deadline | Day of the week |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 6 April to 5 July 2027 | 7 August 2027 | Saturday |
| 2 | 6 April to 5 October 2027 | 7 November 2027 | Sunday |
| 3 | 6 April to 5 January 2028 | 7 February 2028 | Monday |
| 4 | 6 April 2027 to 5 April 2028 | 7 May 2028 | Sunday |

Each update is cumulative, so it covers the year so far, not just the last three months.

## Other dates around it

- **31 January 2027:** file your 2025-26 Self Assessment return the usual way.
- **6 April 2027:** MTD starts and you begin keeping digital records.
- **31 January 2028:** file your 2026-27 return the usual way.
- **31 January 2029:** final declaration for 2027-28, the year you used MTD.

## Weekends

Three of the four deadlines fall on a weekend. Do not rely on a weekend allowance. Treat the Friday before as your real deadline, or send the update as soon as the quarter ends.

## Calendar update periods

You can choose calendar quarters instead of the standard ones, which end on 5 July, 5 October, 5 January and 5 April. GOV.UK's checklist notes that records start from 1 April under that option. Check the guidance before choosing, because it affects how you line up your bookkeeping.

## Penalties

A points-based system applies to late updates. The rules differ by when you join, so read HMRC's penalty guidance on GOV.UK rather than relying on a summary.

## Make it routine

1. Put the four dates in your calendar with a reminder two weeks before.
2. Reconcile your income and expenses monthly, so each update takes minutes.
3. Send each update early, then check the confirmation in your software.

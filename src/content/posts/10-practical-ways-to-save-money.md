## 1. Create a realistic budget

Track what comes in and what goes out so you can see where your money goes. Split it into essentials, savings and spending you choose. A spreadsheet or a budgeting app both work.

## 2. Automate your savings

Set up a standing order to a savings account on payday. Money you never see is money you do not spend.

## 3. Cut unused subscriptions

Check your bank statements for streaming, software and app subscriptions you no longer use. Cancel them, or share family plans where the terms allow.

## 4. Compare prices before you buy

Use price comparison sites, cashback and discount codes. Wait a day before impulse purchases, and consider second-hand or refurbished electronics.

## 5. Cook at home and plan meals

Meal planning cuts food waste and spending, and usually makes it easier to eat well.

## 6. Pay off high-interest debt first

Clear the debts with the highest interest rate first, such as credit cards and overdrafts. Free, impartial help is available from MoneyHelper.

## 7. Lower your energy bills

LED bulbs, efficient appliances and a smart thermostat add up over a year. Compare your tariff when your fixed deal ends.

## 8. Use rewards sensibly

Cashback and loyalty points are worth having only if you would buy the item anyway and you pay any card balance in full.

## 9. Use tax-free and tax-efficient accounts

An ISA lets you save or invest up to £20,000 a year free of UK tax. Pensions add tax relief on contributions. Read our guide to why you [do not need to rush into a cash ISA](/money/cash-isa-limit-stays-20000/) and what happens to [tax on savings interest from April 2027](/money/tax-on-savings-interest-april-2027/).

## 10. Review regularly

Revisit your budget every few months. Income, prices and goals change, and your plan should too.
